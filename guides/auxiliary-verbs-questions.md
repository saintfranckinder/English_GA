# Study Guide: Auxiliary Verbs and Questions in English

---

## 1. What are Auxiliary Verbs?

**Auxiliary verbs** (also called *helping verbs*) work alongside a main verb to express **tense**, **possibility**, **ability**, **obligation**, or to construct **questions** and **negations**.

### The Main Auxiliary Groups

| Group | Forms |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Be** | `am`, `is`, `are`, `was`, `were` |
| **Do** | `do`, `does`, `did` |
| **Have** | `have`, `has`, `had` |
| **Modals** | `can`, `could`, `may`, `might`, `will`, `would`, `shall`, `should`, `must` |

> **Key Concept — Inversion:**  
> In English questions, auxiliary verbs usually move before the subject. This word order shift is called **inversion**.

---

## 2. Yes/No Questions

A **Yes/No question** is a closed question that can usually be answered with a simple "yes" or "no".

### Basic Pattern

$$\text{Auxiliary Verb} + \text{Subject} + \text{Main Verb} + \dots ?$$

### Examples
* **Do** you *work* on Saturdays?
* **Does** she *speak* French?
* **Did** they *arrive* early?
* **Are** you *studying* English?
* **Have** you *finished* the report?
* **Can** he *help* us?

### Short Answers
Short answers repeat the **auxiliary verb**, not the entire verb phrase:
* **Q:** *Do you speak English?*  
  $\rightarrow$ **A:** Yes, I **do**. / No, I **don't**.
* **Q:** *Is she studying?*  
  $\rightarrow$ **A:** Yes, she **is**. / No, she **isn't**.

---

## 3. Using *Do*, *Does*, and *Did*

English uses **do / does / did** when a sentence is in the **simple present** or **simple past** and contains **no other auxiliary verb**.

### Sentence Patterns

#### Simple Present
* **Do** $+ \text{(I / you / we / they)} + \text{Base Verb}?$
  * *Example:* **Do** they *live* in Haiti?
* **Does** $+ \text{(he / she / it)} + \text{Base Verb}?$
  * *Example:* **Does** your brother *like* coffee?

#### Simple Past
* **Did** $+ \text{Subject} + \text{Base Verb}?$ *(Same for all subjects)*
  * *Example:* **Did** you *call* your client yesterday?
  * *Example:* **Did** she *go* to school?

> ### ⚠️ Crucial Rule
> After **do**, **does**, or **did**, always use the **base form** (bare infinitive) of the main verb. The auxiliary verb already marks the tense and person, so the main verb **does not take** $-s$ or $-ed$.
>
> | Status | Example |
> | :--- | :--- |
> | **Correct** | **Does** she **work** here? |
> | ~~Incorrect~~ | ~~Does she works here?~~ |
> | **Correct** | **Did** they **go** home? |
> | ~~Incorrect~~ | ~~Did they went home?~~ |

---

## 4. Questions with *Be*

Use **be** directly at the start of a question when it acts as the **main verb** or forms a **continuous tense**.

### Pattern
$$\text{Be} + \text{Subject} + \text{Complement / Verb-ing}?$$

### Examples

#### As Main Verb
* **Are** you ready?
* **Is** he a teacher?
* **Were** they at the meeting?

#### In Continuous Tenses
* **Are** you working today?
* **Is** she preparing a presentation?
* **Were** they waiting outside?

> **Rule:** Never add *do / does / did* before the verb *be*.
> * **Correct:** Are you tired?
> * ~~**Incorrect:** Do you are tired?~~

---

## 5. Questions with *Have*

### Perfect Tenses
Use **have / has / had** at the beginning of a sentence in perfect tenses:

$$\text{Have / Has / Had} + \text{Subject} + \text{Past Participle}?$$

* **Have** you *completed* the application?
* **Has** she *arrived* yet?
* **Had** they *left* before noon?

### Possession
When **have** indicates possession (especially in American English), form questions using **do / does / did**:
* **Do** you *have* a passport?
* **Does** he *have* a new phone?
* **Did** they *have* enough money?

---

## 6. Questions with Modal Verbs

**Modal verbs** express ability, advice, possibility, permission, prediction, or obligation.

### Pattern
$$\text{Modal Verb} + \text{Subject} + \text{Base Verb}?$$

### Examples
* **Can** you help me?
* **Should** we invest in this project?
* **Will** they attend the meeting?
* **Must** employees wear an ID badge?

> **Rule:** Never combine *do / does / did* with a modal verb.
> * **Correct:** Can she drive?
> * ~~**Incorrect:** Does she can drive?~~

---

## 7. WH- Questions

Use a **question word** (Wh- word) when requesting specific information rather than a simple Yes/No response.

### Common Question Words

| Word | Function / Focus |
| :--- | :--- |
| **What** | Things or actions |
| **Where** | Places |
| **When** | Time |
| **Who** | People |
| **Why** | Reasons |
| **Which** | Specific choice |
| **How** | Method, condition, quantity, or degree |

### Common Pattern
$$\text{Question Word} + \text{Auxiliary Verb} + \text{Subject} + \text{Main Verb}?$$

### Examples
* **Where** *do* you live?
* **What** *does* she study?
* **When** *did* the program begin?
* **Why** *are* they leaving?
* **How** *have* you learned English?
* **Which** course *will* you choose?

---

## 8. Subject Questions vs. Object Questions

A **subject question** asks *who* or *what* performs the action. Because the question word is the subject, **do not** use *do / does / did*.

### Subject Question Pattern
$$\text{Who / What / Which} + \text{Main Verb (conjugated)} + \dots ?$$

* **Who** called you?
* **What** happened yesterday?
* **Which company** offers this service?

### Direct Comparison

| Type | Question | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subject Question** | *Who called Marie?* | "Who" is performing the action (the subject). |
| **Object Question** | *Who did Marie call?* | "Marie" is performing the action; "Who" receives it (the object). |

---

## 9. Common Errors to Avoid

| Error Type | Incorrect | Correct | Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Double Auxiliaries** | ~~Do you can speak English?~~ | **Can** you speak English? | Don't add *do* to modal verbs. |
| **Past Tense after *Did*** | ~~Did she studied economics?~~ | Did she **study** economics? | Always use the **base verb** after *did*. |
| **Incorrect Word Order** | ~~You are working today?~~ | **Are you** working today? | Invert subject and auxiliary verb. |
| **Subject Agreement** | ~~Does they work here?~~ | **Do** they work here? | Use *does* only for *he/she/it*. |
| **Adding *Do* to *Be*** | ~~Do you are ready?~~ | **Are** you ready? | Never use *do* with the verb *be*. |

## 10. Quick Recall Practice

### Turn these statements into questions:

1. **Statement:** She studies economics.
2. **Statement:** They visited the office.
3. **Statement:** You are preparing a project.
4. **Statement:** He can speak French.
5. **Statement:** The team has finished its work.

---

### Practice Answers & Solution Explanation

#### Answer Key:
1. **Does she study economics?**
2. **Did they visit the office?**
3. **Are you preparing a project?**
4. **Can he speak French?**
5. **Has the team finished its work?**

#### Explanation for Question #1:
> **Question:** What auxiliary would you choose first for the sentence *"She studies economics"*, and why?
> 
> **Answer:** You choose **Does**.  
> **Reason:** 
> 1. **Tense:** The verb *studies* is in the **Simple Present** tense.
> 2. **No existing auxiliary:** There is no auxiliary verb (*be*, *have*, or a modal) present in the statement.
> 3. **Subject Agreement:** The subject is third-person singular (*she*), which requires **does** instead of *do*. 
> 4. **Verb form adjustment:** Once **does** is introduced, *studies* reverts to its base form: **study**.
