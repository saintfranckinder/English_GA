# Study Guide: Relative Clauses in English

---

## 1. What Are Relative Clauses?

**Relative clauses** are dependent clauses that provide additional information about a noun (called the *antecedent*) in the main clause. 

### Why Use Them?
* They help avoid repetition.
* They make writing and speech more concise, cohesive, and fluent.
* They are introduced by **relative pronouns** or **relative adverbs**.

---

## 2. Relative Pronouns and Adverbs

| Pronoun / Adverb | Refers To | Function | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **who** | People (sometimes pets) | Subject or Object | *The woman **who** lives next door is a doctor.* |
| **whom** | People | Object (Formal) | *The man **whom** you met is my uncle.* |
| **whose** | People, animals, things | Possession | *The student **whose** project won is from Haiti.* |
| **which** | Animals, things, whole clauses | Subject or Object | *The book, **which** is on the table, is mine.* |
| **that** | People, animals, things | Subject or Object (Informal; defining only) | *The car **that** I bought is red.* |
| **where** | Places | Location | *This is the school **where** I studied.* |
| **when** | Times | Time | *I remember the day **when** we met.* |
| **why** | Reasons | Reason | *That's the reason **why** I left.* |

### Key Grammar Rules

* **Who vs. Whom:** Use `who` when the relative pronoun is the *subject* of the clause (replaceable by *he/she/they*). Use `whom` when it is the *object* (replaceable by *him/her/them*), especially in formal writing.
* **Possession:** Use `whose` to show ownership for people, animals, or things.
* **That:** Use `that` **only** in defining (restrictive) clauses. Never use `that` in non-defining clauses.
* **Which:** Use `which` for things and animals. It can also refer back to an entire preceding sentence or clause.

---

## 3. Types of Relative Clauses

### A. Defining (Restrictive) Relative Clauses
Defining relative clauses provide **essential information** needed to identify the noun they modify. Without this clause, the sentence is incomplete or unclear.

* **Commas:** None.
* **Pronouns Allowed:** `who`, `whom`, `whose`, `which`, `that`, `where`, `when`.
* **Omission:** The relative pronoun can be omitted if it is the **object** of the relative clause (e.g., *"The book [that] I read"*).

> **Examples:**
> * *The man **who called you** is my brother.* (Specifies *which* man)
> * *The book **that I borrowed** is interesting.* (Specifies *which* book)
> * *The city **where I was born** is Port-au-Prince.*

---

### B. Non-Defining (Non-Restrictive) Relative Clauses
Non-defining relative clauses add **extra, non-essential information**. The main sentence still makes complete sense if you remove the clause.

* **Commas:** Always separated by commas (or parentheses).
* **Pronouns Allowed:** `who`, `whom`, `whose`, `which`, `where`, `when` (**Never use `that`**).
* **Omission:** The relative pronoun **cannot** be omitted.

> **Examples:**
> * *My brother, **who lives in Miami**, is visiting next week.* (Extra detail about my brother)
> * *The Eiffel Tower, **which is in Paris**, is a famous landmark.*
> * *Dr. Jean, **whose research focuses on microfinance**, published a new paper.*

---

## 4. Quick Decision Guide: Which Pronoun to Use?

```
Is the antecedent a person?
  ├─ Subject ───────► who (or "that" in defining clauses)
  ├─ Object ────────► whom (formal) / who / that
  └─ Possession ────► whose

Is the antecedent a thing or animal?
  ├─ Defining ──────► that OR which
  └─ Non-Defining ──► which ONLY

Does it refer to an entire sentence?
  └─────────────────► which ONLY 
                     (Example: "She passed the exam, which surprised everyone.")

Is the information essential to identifying the noun?
  ├─ Essential (Defining) ─────► NO commas | "that" IS allowed
  └─ Extra (Non-defining) ────► COMMAS required | "that" NOT allowed
```

## 5. Common Mistakes to Avoid

* ❌ **Using "that" in non-defining clauses**
  * *Wrong:* My teacher, that is from Canada, speaks French.
  * *Right:* My teacher, **who** is from Canada, speaks French.
* ❌ **Adding unnecessary commas in defining clauses**
  * *Wrong:* The book, that I read, was interesting.
  * *Right:* The book **that** I read was interesting.
* ❌ **Using "which" for people**
  * *Wrong:* The woman which called is my aunt.
  * *Right:* The woman **who** called is my aunt.
* ❌ **Omitting the pronoun in non-defining clauses**
  * *Wrong:* My friend, lives in Pétionville, is a marketer.
  * *Right:* My friend, **who** lives in Pétionville, is a marketer.

## 6. Quick Reference Summary Table

| Feature | Defining Clause | Non-Defining Clause |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Information** | Essential | Extra / Optional |
| **Commas Used?** | No | Yes |
| **Allowed Pronouns** | `who`, `whom`, `whose`, `which`, `that`, `where`, `when` | `who`, `whom`, `whose`, `which`, `where`, `when` |
| **Is "that" allowed?** | Yes | **No** |
| **Can omit pronoun?** | Yes (if object of clause) | **No** |
| **Example** | *The book **that** I bought is new.* | *This book, **which** I bought, is new.* |

## 7. Practice Exercises

### A. Identify the Type
*Label each clause as Defining (**D**) or Non-Defining (**ND**):*

1. The project that we launched last month is successful.
2. My colleague, who specializes in AI, gave a presentation.
3. The microfinance institution which operates in Haiti serves 10,000 clients.
4. The SDGs, which were adopted in 2015, guide global development.

---

### B. Choose the Correct Pronoun
*Fill in the blanks with `who`, `whom`, `whose`, `which`, `that`, `where`, or `when`:*

1. The student ________ proposal was selected will present tomorrow.
2. This is the market ________ I buy my supplies.
3. The report, ________ was published yesterday, contains new data.
4. The entrepreneur ________ you interviewed is from Gonaïves.

---

### C. Combine the Sentences
*Combine the two sentences into one using a relative clause:*

1. I met a woman. She works in digital marketing.
2. The course is online. It focuses on sustainable development.
3. Port-au-Prince is the capital. I was born there.

---

### 🔑 Answer Key

* **Exercise A:** 1-D, 2-ND, 3-D, 4-ND
* **Exercise B:** 1-whose, 2-where, 3-which, 4-whom (or who)
* **Exercise C:**
  1. *I met a woman **who** works in digital marketing.*
  2. *The course **that / which** focuses on sustainable development is online.*
  3. *Port-au-Prince, **where** I was born, is the capital.*

---

## 8. Tips for Mastery

1. **Read Aloud:** Read sentences out loud and pause at commas to determine if the clause is extra (non-defining) or essential (defining).
2. **Formality Context:** Use `whom` for objects in formal or academic contexts.
3. **Quick Check for Things:** When referring to objects, default to `which` for non-defining clauses with commas, and `that` for defining clauses without commas.
