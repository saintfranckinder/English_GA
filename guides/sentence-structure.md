# Study Guide: Sentence Structure & Word Order in English

English follows a relatively fixed word order compared to many other languages. Mastering these patterns will help you write and speak clearly and correctly.

---

## 1. Basic Sentence Pattern: SVO

The core word order in English is **Subject + Verb + Object** ($S + V + O$).

* **Subject (S):** Who or what performs the action
* **Verb (V):** The action or state
* **Object (O):** Who or what receives the action

### Examples
* She ($S$) reads ($V$) books ($O$).
* The company ($S$) launched ($V$) a new product ($O$).

> **Note:** This order is strict in simple statements. Changing it often makes the sentence incorrect or alters the meaning.

---

## 2. Elements Around the Core SVO

Once you have established the core $S + V + O$ structure, you can add other elements in predictable positions.

### 2.1. Adjectives (Before Nouns)

Adjectives usually come directly before the noun they describe.

* a red car (*not:* a car red)
* an interesting project

When using multiple adjectives, follow this typical order:

$$\text{Opinion} \rightarrow \text{Size} \rightarrow \text{Age} \rightarrow \text{Shape} \rightarrow \text{Color} \rightarrow \text{Origin} \rightarrow \text{Material} \rightarrow \text{Purpose} + \text{Noun}$$

* **Example:** a beautiful small old round brown French wooden table

> **Key Rule:** Opinion adjectives always come before factual ones.

---

### 2.2. Adverbs (Position Depends on Type)

Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. Their position depends on their specific role in the sentence.

#### Common Positions
1. **Front of the Clause:** Used for emphasis, attitude, or time.
   * *Yesterday*, she finished the report.
   * *Unfortunately*, the meeting was cancelled.
2. **Mid Position:** Located between the subject and main verb, after the first auxiliary, or after the verb *to be*.
   * She *often* travels for work.
   * He has *already* submitted the application.
   * She is *always* punctual.
3. **End of the Clause:** Common with manner adverbs.
   * He speaks English *fluently*.
   * They worked *carefully*.

#### Important Rules
* **Never** place an adverb between a verb and its direct object.
  * ❌ *She reads carefully the report.*
  * ✅ *She reads the report carefully.*
* Adverbs modifying adjectives or other adverbs go directly before them (e.g., *very good*, *extremely quickly*).

---

## 3. Time and Place Expressions

When a sentence contains both place and time expressions at the end, the standard sequence is **Place before Time**.

$$\text{Place} \rightarrow \text{Time}$$

* We met **at the office** ($\text{Place}$) **yesterday** ($\text{Time}$).
* She will present the project **in Port-au-Prince** ($\text{Place}$) **next week** ($\text{Time}$).

> **Variation:** You can move time expressions to the front for emphasis (e.g., *"Yesterday, we met at the office."*).

---

## 4. Negative Sentences

In negative sentences, follow this basic pattern:

$$\text{Subject} + \text{Auxiliary/Modal} + \text{not} + \text{Main Verb} + \text{Object}$$

* I **do not understand** the lesson.
* She **has not finished** her assignment.
* They **cannot attend** the meeting.

### With "to be" as the Main Verb

$$\text{Subject} + \text{to be} + \text{not}$$

* He **is not** ready.
* They **were not** at home.

### Adverbs in Negative Sentences
Adverbs in negatives usually sit after the auxiliary verb and before the main verb:
* She does **not often** travel.

---

## 5. Questions (Word Order Changes)

English questions require subject-verb inversion or auxiliary placement.

### 5.1. Yes/No Questions

$$\text{Auxiliary/Modal} + \text{Subject} + \text{Main Verb} + \dots ?$$

* **Do** you **understand** the lesson?
* **Has** she **submitted** the report?
* **Can** they **join** the meeting?

#### With "to be":
* **Are** you ready?
* **Was** he at the office?

---

### 5.2. Wh- Questions

$$\text{Wh-word} + \text{Auxiliary/Modal} + \text{Subject} + \text{Main Verb} + \dots ?$$

* **Where** do you work?
* **What** has she studied?
* **When** will they arrive?

> **Exception:** If the Wh-word is the subject of the sentence, there is no subject-auxiliary inversion:
> * **Who** called you? (*who* = subject)
> * **What** happened? (*what* = subject)

---

## 6. Common Sentence Patterns

| Pattern | Structure Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$S + V$** | Subject + Verb | She sleeps. |
| **$S + V + O$** | Subject + Verb + Direct Object | He reads articles. |
| **$S + V + IO + DO$** | Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object | She gave him a book. |
| **$S + V + O + C$** | Subject + Verb + Direct Object + Object Complement | They elected her president. |
| **$S + LV + Adj$** | Subject + Linking Verb + Adjective | The project seems feasible. |
| **$S + LV + N$** | Subject + Linking Verb + Noun | He became a manager. |

> **Linking Verbs:** Verbs such as *be, become, seem, appear, feel, look, sound, taste, smell* are followed by adjectives (not adverbs) when describing the subject.
> * ✅ *The plan sounds good.*
> * ❌ *The plan sounds goodly.*

---

## 7. Typical Mistakes for French Speakers

Because French word order is sometimes more flexible, watch out for these common errors:

| Category | Incorrect Pattern | Correct Pattern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Adjective Position** | ❌ *a car red* | ✅ *a red car* |
| **Adverb Placement** | ❌ *I read carefully the text.* | ✅ *I read the text carefully.* |
| **Place vs. Time Order** | ❌ *We met yesterday at the office.* | ✅ *We met at the office yesterday.* |
| **Question Inversion** | ❌ *You do understand?* | ✅ *Do you understand?* |

## 8. Quick Practice Exercises

1. **Reorder Scrambled Words:**
   * *Prompt:* books / she / reads / often
   * *Answer:* She often reads books.

2. **Transform Statement into Question and Negative:**
   * *Statement:* He works in marketing.
   * *Question:* Does he work in marketing?
   * *Negative:* He does not work in marketing.

3. **Add Adjectives and Adverbs Correctly:**
   * *Start:* The company launched a product.
   * *Expanded:* The innovative company recently launched a new digital product successfully.

4. **Rewrite with Place and Time in Correct Order:**
   * *Prompt:* We will have a meeting tomorrow in Pétionville.
   * *Answer:* We will have a meeting in Pétionville tomorrow.
