# Study Guide: Verb Tenses and Aspect

**Scope:** This guide describes common English verb forms and their uses. It distinguishes tense from aspect and avoids treating every future-time construction as a grammatical future tense. In English, verbs have present and past tense forms; future time is expressed in several ways, including *will*, *be going to*, and present-tense forms.

---

## 1. Key Concepts

### Tense
Tense refers to the grammatical form of a verb that commonly relates an event or state to time.

* **Present forms:** work, works, am working
* **Past forms:** worked, was working

English does not have one distinct future-tense verb form. Instead, speakers use several constructions to refer to future time.

### Aspect
Aspect describes how an action, event, or state is viewed in relation to time.

| Aspect | Main Idea | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple** | An event, action, or state is presented without marking it as ongoing or completed in relation to another time. | She works. |
| **Continuous / Progressive** | An action is viewed as ongoing or in progress. | She is working. |
| **Perfect** | A situation is viewed from a later reference point. | She has worked. |
| **Perfect Continuous** | A situation is viewed as ongoing up to a reference point. | She has been working. |

The continuous aspect uses a form of *be* plus an *-ing* form. The perfect aspect uses a form of *have* plus a past participle.

## 3. Present Forms

### Present Simple
**Form:**
* I / you / we / they **work**
* He / she / it **works**

**Common uses:**
* **States:** I know the answer.
* **Habits or repeated actions:** She walks to school.
* **General facts:** Water boils at 100°C at sea level.
* **Timetabled or scheduled events:** The train leaves at six.

**Examples:**
* They study English.
* He lives in Toronto.
* The class begins at nine.

---

### Present Continuous
**Form:**
* am / is / are + **-ing form**

**Examples:**
* I am reading.
* She is reading.
* They are reading.

**Common uses:**
* **An action in progress:** She is talking on the phone.
* **A temporary situation:** I am staying with my cousin this week.
* **A developing or changing situation:** The weather is getting colder.

---

### Present Perfect
**Form:**
* have / has + **past participle**

**Examples:**
* I have finished.
* She has finished.
* They have written the report.

**Common uses:**
* **An experience up to the present:** I have visited Japan.
* **A past situation that continues into the present:** She has lived here for five years.
* **A past event with a present result:** He has lost his keys.

---

### Present Perfect Continuous
**Form:**
* have / has been + **-ing form**

**Examples:**
* I have been studying.
* He has been working.
* They have been waiting.

**Common uses:**
* **An activity that began in the past and continues now:** I have been studying since noon.
* **The duration of an activity:** She has been working for three hours.
* **A recently continuing activity with a visible or relevant result:** They are tired because they have been running.

---

## 4. Past Forms

### Past Simple
**Form:**
* Regular verb: **worked**
* Irregular verb: **went**, **saw**, **wrote**

**Common uses:**
* **A completed past event:** We watched a film last night.
* **A series of completed events:** She opened the door, walked in, and sat down.
* **A past state or habit:** He lived there as a child.

**Examples:**
* I finished my homework.
* They went to the museum.
* She knew the answer.

---

### Past Continuous
**Form:**
* was / were + **-ing form**

**Examples:**
* I was reading.
* She was reading.
* They were reading.

**Common uses:**
* **An action in progress at a particular past time:** At eight o’clock, I was studying.
* **A background action:** The sun was shining when we left.
* **An action interrupted by another event:** I was cooking when the phone rang.
* **Two actions in progress at the same time:** She was reading while he was writing.

---

### Past Perfect
**Form:**
* had + **past participle**

**Examples:**
* I had finished.
* She had left.
* They had written the report.

**Common uses:**
* Looks back from a time in the past.
  * When I arrived, the film had started.
  * She was tired because she had worked all day.
  * They had already eaten when we called.

---

### Past Perfect Continuous
**Form:**
* had been + **-ing form**

**Examples:**
* I had been studying.
* He had been working.
* They had been waiting.

**Common uses:**
* **An activity continuing up to a past reference point:** She had been studying before dinner.
* **The duration of an earlier activity:** They had been waiting for an hour when the bus arrived.
* **An earlier activity that explains a past result:** He was tired because he had been running.

---

## 5. Future-Time Forms

English uses several constructions for future time rather than one single future-tense form.

### Will
**Form:**
* will + **base form**

**Examples:**
* I will call you.
* She will arrive soon.
* They will help us.

**Common uses:**
* Predictions, decisions made at the time of speaking, promises, and offers:
  * I think it will rain.
  * I’ll answer the phone.
  * I will help you.

---

### Be Going To
**Form:**
* am / is / are going to + **base form**

**Examples:**
* I am going to study.
* He is going to travel.
* They are going to move.

**Common uses:**
* **Plans or intentions:** We are going to start tomorrow.
* **Predictions based on present evidence:** Look at those clouds. It is going to rain.

---

### Present Continuous for Future Arrangements
Used to refer to a planned arrangement, often with a future time expression.

* I am meeting my tutor tomorrow.
* They are leaving on Friday.
* She is visiting her grandparents next week.

---

### Present Simple for Schedules and Timetables
Used to refer to scheduled future events.

* The bus leaves at 7:30 tomorrow.
* The semester begins next Monday.
* The meeting starts at noon.

---

## 6. Future-Time Aspect Forms

### Future Continuous
**Form:**
* will be + **-ing form**

**Examples:**
* This time tomorrow, I will be flying.
* At eight tonight, she will be studying.

Presents an activity as ongoing at a future time.

---

### Future Perfect
**Form:**
* will have + **past participle**

**Examples:**
* By Friday, I will have finished the project.
* They will have arrived by noon.

Looks back from a future time and presents an event as completed by that time.

---

### Future Perfect Continuous
**Form:**
* will have been + **-ing form**

**Examples:**
* By June, I will have been working here for two years.
* At noon, she will have been studying for four hours.

Presents an activity as continuing up to a future reference point and often emphasizes duration.

---

## 7. Choosing Between Similar Forms

### Past Simple vs. Present Perfect
* Use **past simple** when referring to a finished past time:
  * I visited Spain in 2022.
  * She finished the book yesterday.
* Use **present perfect** when connecting a past event or situation to the present, without focusing on a finished past time:
  * I have visited Spain.
  * She has finished the book.

### Present Simple vs. Present Continuous
* Use **present simple** for habits, general facts, and states:
  * He plays tennis every Saturday.
  * I understand the question.
* Use **present continuous** for actions in progress or temporary situations:
  * He is playing tennis now.
  * I am staying with friends this week.

### Present Perfect vs. Present Perfect Continuous
* **Present perfect** can emphasize completion, result, or experience:
  * I have written three emails.
* **Present perfect continuous** can emphasize activity or duration:
  * I have been writing emails all morning.

*Note: Some verbs are more naturally used in simple forms when they describe states:*
* I have known her for years. (Not usually: *I have been knowing her for years.*)

---

## 8. Stative Verbs

Stative verbs describe states rather than physical activities. They do not normally appear in continuous forms.

**Common examples:**
* know
* believe
* understand
* want
* need
* own
* belong
* like
* love

**Examples:**
* I understand the lesson.
* She owns a bicycle.
* They believe him.

**Verbs with stative and dynamic meanings:**
* *I think you are right.* (opinion or belief)
* *I am thinking about the problem.* (mental activity in progress)

---

## 9. Negative and Question Forms

### Negatives
Place **not** after an auxiliary verb.

* **Present simple:** She does not work here.
* **Past simple:** They did not leave.
* **Present continuous:** He is not sleeping.
* **Present perfect:** I have not finished.
* **Future with will:** We will not forget.

**Common contractions:**
* do not → don’t
* does not → doesn’t
* did not → didn’t
* is not → isn’t
* have not → haven’t
* will not → won’t

### Questions
Place the auxiliary verb before the subject.

* **Present simple:** Do you work here?
* **Past simple:** Did she call?
* **Present continuous:** Are they waiting?
* **Present perfect:** Have you finished?
* **Future with will:** Will he come?

*With the verb **be**, do not add **do**:*
* Are you ready?
* Was she working?

## 10. Form-Building Chart

| Time Reference / Aspect | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect Continuous |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | I work | I am working | I have worked | I have been working |
| **Past** | I worked | I was working | I had worked | I had been working |
| **Future time (will)** | I will work | I will be working | I will have worked | I will have been working |

---

## 11. Common Errors to Check

1. Use a past participle after *have/has/had*: **She has written**, not *She has wrote*.
2. Use an *-ing* form after the continuous form of *be*: **They are studying**, not *They are study*.
3. Use *did* with the base form in past simple questions and negatives: **Did he go?** and **He did not go**, not *Did he went*.
4. Use *has* with third-person singular subjects in the present perfect: **He has finished**.
5. Do not use *will* automatically for every future meaning. Consider *be going to*, the present continuous, or the present simple for schedules.
6. Remember that time expressions help, but meaning and context also determine the verb form.

## 13. Quick Review Checklist

Before choosing a verb form, ask yourself:

* What time or reference point am I talking about?
* Is the situation a state, habit, event, or activity?
* Is the activity viewed as ongoing?
* Is it viewed as completed before a reference point?
* Is duration important?
* Am I referring to future time through *will*, *be going to*, an arrangement, or a schedule?
* Does the verb describe a state that is not normally used in the continuous form?
