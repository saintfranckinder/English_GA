# Study Guide: Subject–Verb Agreement in English

**Subject–verb agreement** means that the verb in a sentence must match its subject in **number** (singular or plural) and **person** (first, second, or third). 

In practice, most agreement errors occur:
1. In the simple present tense with third-person singular subjects (*he*, *she*, *it*).
2. With complex subjects (e.g., phrases intervening between subject and verb, compound subjects, collective nouns).

---

## 1. Core Rule

* **Singular subject** $\rightarrow$ **Singular verb**
  * *Example:* The student **studies** every day.
* **Plural subject** $\rightarrow$ **Plural verb**
  * *Example:* The students **study** every day.

> **Note:** In the simple present tense, third-person singular verbs usually end in **-s** or **-es** (e.g., *go* $\rightarrow$ *goes*, *watch* $\rightarrow$ *watches*, *study* $\rightarrow$ *studies*).

---

## 2. Basic Patterns to Memorize

### Present Tense: "To Be" and "To Have"

| Subject | `be` (Present) | `have` (Present) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | am | have |
| **You** | are | have |
| **He / She / It** | is | has |
| **We / You / They** | are | have |

* **Examples:**
  * She **is** ready. / They **are** ready.
  * He **has** a plan. / We **have** a plan.

---

### "Do" / "Does" and Contractions

* **Does / Doesn’t** $\rightarrow$ Singular subjects (*he*, *she*, *it*, *the company*)
* **Do / Don’t** $\rightarrow$ Plural subjects (*they*, *we*, *you*, *the companies*)
* **Exception:** *I* and *you* take **do / don’t** even though they refer to single individuals.

* **Examples:**
  * She **doesn’t** agree.
  * They **don’t** agree.
  * I **don’t** agree. / You **don’t** agree.

---

## 3. Common Tricky Situations

### A. Words Between Subject and Verb
Ignore phrases introduced by prepositions or expressions such as *with*, *along with*, *together with*, *including*, *as well as*, *in addition to*, or *of*. The verb agrees with the **main subject**, not with nouns inside the modifier phrase.

* **Incorrect:** The manager, along with two assistants, *are* traveling.
* **Correct:** The manager, along with two assistants, **is** traveling.
* **Correct:** The reports, including the summary, **were** submitted.
* **Correct:** The color of the walls **is** blue. *(Subject = color)*

---

### B. Compound Subjects Joined by "And"
Two or more subjects joined by `and` generally take a **plural verb**.

* **Example:** Marie and Marc **visit** their grandparents.

> **Exception:** If the joined elements form a single unit or refer to the same person/thing, use a **singular verb**.
> * Bread and butter **is** my favorite snack. *(Treated as a single dish/concept)*
> * The founder and CEO **is** speaking. *(Refers to one person holding both roles)*

---

### C. Compound Subjects Joined by "Or" / "Nor" (*Either...or*, *Neither...nor*)
The verb agrees with the subject component that is **closest to the verb**.

* Either the teacher or the students **are** responsible. *(Closest: "students" $\rightarrow$ plural)*
* Either the students or the teacher **is** responsible. *(Closest: "teacher" $\rightarrow$ singular)*
* Neither the books nor the article **was** useful. *(Closest: "article" $\rightarrow$ singular)*
* Neither the article nor the books **were** useful. *(Closest: "books" $\rightarrow$ plural)*

---

### D. Indefinite Pronouns
The following indefinite pronouns are **always singular** and take singular verbs:
> *each, each one, either, neither, everyone, everybody, anybody, anyone, nobody, somebody, someone, no one*

* Everyone **has** a role.
* Nobody **knows** the answer.
* Each of the participants **receives** a certificate.

#### Variable Indefinite Pronouns (*All, Some, None, Any, Most*)
These can be singular or plural depending on the noun in the prepositional phrase that follows (*of the...*):

* All of the water **is** gone. *(Water = uncountable noun $\rightarrow$ singular)*
* All of the students **are** present. *(Students = plural noun $\rightarrow$ plural)*

---

### E. Collective Nouns
Nouns such as *group*, *team*, *committee*, *family*, *class*, or *organization* describe a group of individuals.

* **American English Standard:** Collective nouns are treated as **singular** when the group acts as a single cohesive unit.
  * The team **wins** its matches.
  * The committee **has** made its decision.
* **Individual Action Exception (or British English style):** Treated as **plural** when members act individually.
  * The team **are** arguing among themselves.

---

### F. Sentences Starting with "There is / are" and "Here is / are"
In sentences beginning with *there* or *here*, the subject follows the verb. Match the verb to the subject that comes after it.

* There **is** a problem. *(Subject = a problem)*
* There **are** many problems. *(Subject = many problems)*
* Here **is** your document. *(Subject = your document)*
* Here **are** your documents. *(Subject = your documents)*

---

### G. Titles, Proper Names, and Units of Measurement
* **Titles & Names:** Names of books, movies, organizations, or countries are singular, even if they end in *-s*.
  * *The United Nations* **is** holding a meeting.
  * *Physics* **is** difficult for many students.
* **Amounts & Quantities:** Distances, periods of time, sums of money, and quantities are treated as a single unit and take a **singular verb**.
  * Ten dollars **is** enough.
  * Five years **is** a long time.
  * Three kilometers **is** not far.

---

### H. Gerunds and Infinitives as Subjects
When a gerund (*-ing* noun form) or an infinitive phrase serves as the subject, use a **singular verb**.

* **Reading** improves your vocabulary.
* **To travel abroad** requires planning.
* **Studying every day** helps you pass.

---

## 4. Special Cases and Notes

### Relative Clauses (*Who, Which, That*)
The verb inside a relative clause must agree with its **antecedent** (the specific noun being described).

* She is one of the students who **study** hard.  
  *(Antecedent = "students" $\rightarrow$ plural verb: study)*
* He is the only one of the students who **studies** hard.  
  *(Antecedent = "the only one" $\rightarrow$ singular verb: studies)*

---

### Inverted Sentence Order
When a sentence places the verb before the subject ( common in questions or after descriptive adverbs), identify the subject carefully.

* On the wall **hang** several posters. *(Subject = several posters)*
* On the wall **hangs** a large poster. *(Subject = a large poster)*

---

### "Each" / "Every" with Compound Subjects
When *each* or *every* precedes subjects joined by *and*, the phrase requires a **singular verb**.

* **Every** teacher and student **has** to register.
* **Each** book and article **was** reviewed.

---

## 5. Quick Decision Checklist

When evaluating subject–verb agreement in a sentence, ask these questions:

1. **What is the true subject?** Cross out intervening phrases (*with...*, *of...*, *in addition to...*).
2. **Is the subject singular or plural?**
   * Joined by *and*? $\rightarrow$ Usually plural.
   * Joined by *or/nor*? $\rightarrow$ Match the noun closest to the verb.
   * Indefinite pronoun (*everyone*, *nobody*)? $\rightarrow$ Singular.
   * Collective noun (*team*, *committee*)? $\rightarrow$ Usually singular.
3. **Is the sentence inverted?** (e.g., *There is/are...*, or prepositions at the start) $\rightarrow$ Locate the subject *after* the verb.
4. **Is it a single unit/amount?** (e.g., titles, money, time, distance) $\rightarrow$ Singular.

1. The list of items **is** on the table.
2. Each of the participants **receives** a certificate.
3. The manager, together with his assistants, **is** traveling tomorrow.
4. Either the teachers or the principal **has** to decide.
5. Neither the principal nor the teachers **are** satisfied.
6. Ten kilometers **is** a long distance to walk.
7. Physics **is** required for this program.

9. The committee **has** already made its decision.

---

## 8. Tips for Ongoing Practice

* **Underline & Circle:** When proofreading your drafts, underline the subject and circle the verb to confirm they match in number.
* **Read Aloud:** Agreement errors often sound unnatural when spoken aloud.
* **Build a Log:** Keep a personal running list of agreement patterns you find challenging and review them periodically.
* **Contextual Application:** Practice writing short, professional summaries of your projects or studies while deliberately checking every verb against its subject.
