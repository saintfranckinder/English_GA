# Comprehensive Study Guide: English Prepositions

> **Definition:** A **preposition** is a word (or group of words) used before a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase to show direction, time, place, location, spatial relationships, or to introduce an object.

---

## 1. Simple Prepositions

### Definition
Simple prepositions are single-word prepositions. They are the most fundamental and frequently used prepositions in English.

### Common Examples
`at`, `by`, `for`, `from`, `in`, `of`, `on`, `to`, `with`, `under`, `over`, `through`, `near`, `between`, `among`, `before`, `after`, `during`, `since`, `until`, `without`, `about`, `against`, `behind`, `beneath`, `beside`, `beyond`, `into`, `onto`, `upon`, `within`, `across`, `along`, `around`, `above`, `below`, `up`, `down`, `off`, `out`, `past`, `toward(s)`.

### Examples in Sentences
* She lives **in** Port-au-Prince.
* The meeting starts **at** 9 a.m.
* He walked **to** the market.
* The book is **on** the table.
* We traveled **by** bus.

---

## 2. Compound (Complex) Prepositions

### Definition
Compound (or complex) prepositions consist of two or more words functioning as a single prepositional unit. They frequently end with simple prepositions like *to*, *of*, *in*, or *on*.

### Common Examples
* `according to`
* `ahead of`
* `along with`
* `as for`
* `because of`
* `by means of`
* `close to`
* `due to`
* `far from`
* `in addition to`
* `in case of`
* `in charge of`
* `in comparison with`
* `in contact with`
* `in favor of`
* `in front of`
* `in need of`
* `in place of`
* `in reference to`
* `in relation to`
* `in return for`
* `in spite of`
* `in view of`
* `instead of`
* `next to`
* `on account of`
* `on behalf of`
* `on top of`
* `opposite to`
* `out of`
* `prior to`
* `subsequent to`
* `together with`
* `up to`
* `with regard to`

### Examples in Sentences
* **According to** the report, sales increased.
* She succeeded **in spite of** the difficulties.
* He left **because of** the rain.
* The office is **in front of** the bank.
* We acted **on behalf of** the community.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you can replace a multi-word phrase with a single preposition and the sentence maintains its meaning, it is a compound preposition.
> * *Example:* "in front of" → "before" (*The office is before the bank.*)

## 3. Prepositions of Time

Prepositions of time indicate **when** an event or action occurs.

### 3.1 Main Prepositions: `at`, `on`, `in`

| Preposition | Core Usage | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **at** | Exact times, specific points in time, fixed expressions | *at 7:00*, *at noon*, *at midnight*, *at sunrise*, *at night*, *at the weekend* (BrE), *at Christmas* |
| **on** | Days and dates, specific days with parts of the day | *on Monday*, *on 25 December*, *on my birthday*, *on Friday morning*, *on New Year’s Day* |
| **in** | Longer periods: months, years, centuries, seasons, parts of the day | *in January*, *in 2026*, *in the 21st century*, *in summer*, *in the morning/afternoon/evening* |

### 3.2 Other Common Time Prepositions
* **before** – earlier than a time/event
  > *Finish your work **before** 5 p.m.*
* **after** – later than a time/event
  > *We will meet **after** class.*
* **during** – throughout a period
  > *She slept **during** the movie.*
* **for** – duration (how long)
  > *He studied **for** two hours.*
* **since** – from a specific point in the past until now
  > *I have lived here **since** 2020.*
* **until / till** – up to a certain time
  > *Wait here **until** tomorrow.*
* **by** – not later than; at or before a specified time
  > *Submit the report **by** Friday.*
* **from … to** – starting and ending times
  > *The course runs **from** June **to** August.*
* **within** – inside a specific period of time
  > *Please reply **within** 24 hours.*

## 4. Prepositions of Place

Prepositions of place indicate **where** someone or something is located.

### 4.1 Main Prepositions: `at`, `on`, `in`

| Preposition | Core Usage | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **at** | Specific points, addresses, events, meeting places | *at the door*, *at the station*, *at 25 Rue Lamarre*, *at school*, *at work*, *at home*, *at the party* |
| **on** | Surfaces, streets, floors, public transport, islands | *on the table*, *on the wall*, *on Main Street*, *on the third floor*, *on the bus*, *on the plane*, *on the island* |
| **in** | Enclosed spaces, cities, countries, continents, rooms, areas | *in the box*, *in the room*, *in Port-au-Prince*, *in Haiti*, *in Latin America*, *in the kitchen*, *in the car* |

### 4.2 Other Common Place Prepositions
* **above** – higher than (not touching direct contact)
  > *The picture is **above** the sofa.*
* **below** – lower than (not touching direct contact)
  > *The temperature is **below** zero.*
* **over** – directly above, covering, or across
  > *The bridge goes **over** the river.*
* **under** – directly below or covered by
  > *The cat is **under** the table.*
* **beneath** – under (more formal)
  > *They found water **beneath** the ground.*
* **behind** – at the back of
  > *The garden is **behind** the house.*
* **in front of** – opposite of behind
  > *There is a tree **in front of** the school.*
* **between** – in the middle of two distinct things/people
  > *She sat **between** John and Marie.*
* **among** – in the middle of many or an indistinct group (more than two)
  > *He walked **among** the crowd.*
* **near / close to** – short distance away
  > *We live **near** the market.* / *We live **close to** the market.*
* **next to** – beside; adjacent to
  > *The pharmacy is **next to** the bank.*
* **opposite** – facing; on the other side of an open area
  > *The café is **opposite** the post office.*
* **inside** – within the interior of
  > *Please wait **inside** the building.*
* **outside** – exterior to
  > *The children are playing **outside** the house.*
* **against** – touching and supported by a surface
  > *The ladder is **against** the wall.*
* **around / round** – surrounding or moving in various parts of
  > *They walked **around** the park.*

## 5. Prepositions of Movement (Direction)

Prepositions of movement express direction or motion toward or away from a location.

### Common Prepositions of Movement
* **to** – movement toward a destination
  > *She goes **to** school every day.*
* **into** – entering an enclosed space (movement from outside to inside)
  > *He walked **into** the room.*
* **out of** – leaving an enclosed space (movement from inside to outside)
  > *They ran **out of** the building.*
* **from** – starting point of movement
  > *We traveled **from** Cap-Haïtien to Port-au-Prince.*
* **toward(s)** – in the direction of
  > *She walked **toward** the sea.*
* **through** – passing from one side to the other inside a three-dimensional space
  > *We drove **through** the tunnel.*
* **across** – passing from one side to another across a two-dimensional surface
  > *They swam **across** the river.*
* **along** – moving in a continuous line following a path
  > *We walked **along** the beach.*
* **up** – moving to a higher level
  > *He climbed **up** the hill.*
* **down** – moving to a lower level
  > *She ran **down** the stairs.*
* **past** – going by or beyond a point
  > *He walked **past** the bank without stopping.*
* **onto** – moving to a position on top of a surface
  > *The cat jumped **onto** the roof.*
* **off** – moving away or down from a surface
  > *He fell **off** the chair.*

> 📌 **Key Distinction Notes:**
> * **`in` vs. `into`**: `in` indicates position (*She is in the room*); `into` indicates movement (*She walked into the room*).
> * **`on` vs. `onto`**: `on` indicates position (*The book is on the desk*); `onto` indicates movement (*He put the book onto the desk*).

## 6. Prepositions of Manner

Prepositions of manner describe **how** an action is performed or executed.

* **by** – method, means, or mode of transport
  > *She goes to work **by** bus.*  
  > *He sent the document **by** email.*
* **with** – using an instrument/tool, or accompanying someone/something
  > *She wrote the letter **with** a pen.*  
  > *He came **with** his friend.*
* **in** – language, style, or emotional state
  > *Please speak **in** English.*  
  > *She answered **in** a calm voice.*
* **like** – similar to (informal or figurative comparison)
  > *He runs **like** the wind.*
* **as** – in the capacity or function of
  > *She works **as** a teacher.*
* **without** – lacking or not accompanied by
  > *He left **without** saying goodbye.*

---

## 7. Prepositions of Cause & Reason

These prepositions explain **why** something occurs or what caused an action.

* **because of** – due to; as a result of (neutral/informal)
  > *The match was canceled **because of** the rain.*
* **due to** – caused by (often follows the verb *to be*)
  > *The delay was **due to** traffic.*
* **owing to** – because of (more formal)
  > * **Owing to** bad weather, the flight was delayed.*
* **on account of** – because of (formal)
  > *The event was postponed **on account of** the storm.*
* **from** – originating cause (especially with physical states or emotions)
  > *She suffered **from** stress.*  
  > *He trembled **from** fear.*
* **through** – by means of; as a direct result of
  > *He succeeded **through** hard work.*

> 📝 **Formality Note:**
> * `because of`: Neutral and common in everyday usage.
> * `due to`, `owing to`, `on account of`: Formal and widely preferred in academic or business contexts.

## 9. Practice Ideas for Self-Study

1. **Fill-in-the-Blank Practice**
   * *Sentence 1:* We will meet ___ 6 p.m. ___ Friday.
   * *Sentence 2:* The keys are ___ the table, ___ the kitchen.
2. **Sentence Transformation**
   * Rewrite sentences using a different preposition while preserving the original meaning:
     * *Original:* He arrived before 8. 
     * *Target:* He arrived ___ 8. (*Answer:* **by**)
3. **Error Correction**
   * Read short paragraphs and identify incorrect prepositions (e.g., changing *"I arrived to home"* to *"I arrived home"* or *"I am interested on"* to *"I am interested in"*).
4. **Real-Life Application**
   * Write a short journal entry describing your daily routine, your neighborhood, or a recent travel experience while attempting to use prepositions from every category (time, place, movement, manner, cause).
